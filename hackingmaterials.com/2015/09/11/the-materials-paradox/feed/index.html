Hi,

I’ve only publicly heard of one example making it all the way to commercialization using high-throughput screening – that is new alkaline battery chemistry for Duracell developed as part of a consulting project from UC Berkeley Professor Ceder and Professor Persson (among others). Professor Ceder also had novel high-rate Li-ion battery cathode coating that I heard got to pilot manufacturing stage but could not be synthesized reliably (work published in https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07853?free=2). I also know of another example that made it to a commercial product being sold to other businesses (but not directly to the consumer); it is unfortunately not public information, but was through consulting work with industry rather than pure academia projects later getting picked up by industry.

There has been other modeling work apart from high-throughput screening. Questek Materials has developed several alloys with the aid of modeling that has been picked up by industry. Citrine Informatics has also claimed to have used an ML model to help design new alloys for 3D printing, some information is here (https://citrine.io/first-to-market-high-strength-3d-printable-aluminum-alloy/).

From all the examples I am aware of, one of the major reasons for success has been lots of involvement and tinkering by the experimental groups such that the “final” compound is not always exactly the same as the “initial” prediction. That is, the initial prediction is more of a starting point that still requires a good amount of experimental followup and optimization and requires a lot of “hammering on” by a talented and dedicated experimentalist post prediction.
